# Solar Filament Segmentation Project
## Progress Report: From Dataset Exploration to the First U-Net Baseline

### 1. Executive Summary

Our project aims to automatically detect solar filaments in grayscale solar images using computer vision and deep learning.

So far, we have completed dataset exploration, annotation analysis, a traditional image-processing baseline, data preparation, and our first U-Net segmentation model. We have also evaluated the model across all 217 validation annotation records and inspected its predictions visually.

Our first U-Net achieved a best reported validation Dice score of 0.6563 during training. In the full validation evaluation, it achieved a mean Dice of 0.6310 and a mean IoU of 0.4709 at a probability threshold of 0.5.

The initial baseline is complete. We are now ready to investigate improvements and work toward the final competition submission.

### 2. What We Have Done

#### Phase 1: Understanding the dataset

We began by exploring the dataset to understand the images, annotation formats, and relationships between images and annotations.

The dataset contains:

- 707 physical training images.
- 1,154 annotation records.
- 8,199 polygon annotation objects.
- Original images with a resolution of 2048 × 2048 pixels.

An important discovery was that a single physical image can have multiple annotation records. These records may describe different labeled filament regions.

We therefore grouped records by physical image when splitting the data into training and validation sets. This prevents the same physical image from appearing in both sets.

The resulting split contains 566 training physical images and 141 validation physical images.

#### Phase 2: Visualizing and validating annotations

We created scripts to inspect the annotations and visualize the corresponding filament masks.

This allowed us to understand the polygon representation, compare multiple annotation records for the same image, and check whether the annotations align with the visible solar structures.

These steps were necessary before training because a segmentation model learns directly from the supplied masks. Incorrect masks would lead to incorrect training signals.

#### Phase 3: Building a traditional baseline

Before using deep learning, we tested image-processing techniques based on intensity thresholding and local contrast.

The approach included estimating the solar disk, identifying darker structures, applying morphological operations, and filtering connected components.

Initial thresholding methods performed poorly. A local-contrast approach improved the results on the sampled images, but its performance remained sensitive to parameter choices.

This experiment gave us a traditional baseline and motivated the move toward a learned segmentation model.

#### Phase 4: Preparing the data for deep learning

We developed a preparation pipeline that converts polygon annotations into binary masks.

Each mask labels filament pixels as 1 and background pixels as 0. The images and masks are resized to 512 × 512 pixels to make model training more manageable.

During preparation, we discovered and corrected an annotation-matching bug. The annotations were stored in a separate top-level list rather than inside each image record. We corrected the code to match annotations to images using `image_id`.

The successful preparation run produced 937 training records and 217 validation records, with no skipped records and no empty masks.

We then verified that the prepared images and masks load correctly into PyTorch batches.

#### Phase 5: Implementing and training U-Net

We implemented U-Net, a convolutional neural network designed for pixel-level segmentation.

U-Net has an encoder that learns image features, a decoder that restores spatial detail, and skip connections that help preserve fine spatial information. These characteristics make it a reasonable starting point for detecting curved and narrow solar filaments.

Our model contains approximately 1.94 million parameters and was trained using PyTorch on Apple's MPS acceleration backend.

The initial configuration used 512 × 512 inputs, a batch size of 4, Adam optimization, and a combined binary cross-entropy and Dice loss.

Training ran for 10 epochs. The best checkpoint was saved at epoch 7, with a reported validation Dice score of 0.6563.

Validation performance declined in the final epochs even though training loss continued to improve, suggesting possible overfitting.

#### Phase 6: Visualizing predictions

We generated visual comparisons showing the original solar image, ground-truth mask, predicted mask, and an overlay of the prediction against the ground truth.

The model successfully identifies many major filament structures. However, it also misses some thin regions and predicts extra fragments in certain images.

Eight sampled validation records had a mean Dice score of 0.6465. This was a preliminary visual-analysis sample, not the final full-validation metric.

#### Phase 7: Evaluating the complete validation set

We evaluated the saved U-Net checkpoint across all 217 validation annotation records at probability thresholds of 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, and 0.7.

At threshold 0.5, the results were:

- Mean Dice: 0.6310.
- Median Dice: 0.6591.
- Mean IoU: 0.4709.

The best mean Dice among the tested thresholds was 0.6322 at threshold 0.7, with a mean IoU of 0.4725. This is a small improvement over threshold 0.5, so further threshold tuning is not our highest priority.

### 3. What These Results Mean

The first U-Net is a working baseline, not a final solution.

It has learned meaningful filament patterns and can segment many of the larger structures. However, thin filaments, false-positive fragments, and differences among annotation records remain challenges.

Another important issue is foreground-background imbalance. Filament pixels account for only a small fraction of the pixels in the images. Resizing from 2048 × 2048 to 512 × 512 may also remove fine structures.

The results provide a reference against which we can evaluate future improvements.

The following components have been implemented:

- Annotation visualization tools.
- Traditional threshold-based segmentation baseline.
- Grouped train-validation split.
- Polygon-to-mask preparation pipeline.
- PyTorch dataset loader and verification script.
- U-Net training implementation.
- Saved best U-Net checkpoint.
- Validation prediction visualization.
- Full validation threshold evaluation and CSV results.
- Updated project documentation.

### 5. Next Steps

The next phase will focus on improving segmentation and moving toward the competition submission.

1. Add synchronized image-mask data augmentation, including flips and 90-degree rotations.
2. Introduce early stopping and compare the augmented model against the original baseline.
3. Investigate missed filaments, false positives, and the effects of low resolution.
4. Consider higher-resolution or patch-based training.
5. Compare all experiments using the same validation split and evaluation method.
6. Develop instance-level filament extraction and prepare the competition submission format.

The current model produces a binary filament mask. The final competition pipeline requires individual filament instances, so additional post-processing or instance-level modeling will be necessary.

### 6. Current Milestone

**Completed: First U-Net semantic segmentation baseline.**

We have progressed from understanding the dataset to building, training, visualizing, and evaluating a working deep-learning model.

The next milestone is to improve generalization and segmentation quality, followed by the development of the instance-level competition pipeline.

This report records our current baseline and gives the team a shared starting point for the next phase of development.
